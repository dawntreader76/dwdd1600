Digital Citizenship

What is Digital Citizenship?

Digital citizenship refers to the responsible, safe, and ethical use of technology and the internet. It defines the norms of appropriate behavior when engaging in online communities, consuming content, and managing personal data.

What is Digiatal Law?

Digital Law (or cyber law) is the legal framework governing the use of the internet, digital devices, software, and online data. It establishes legal boundaries for electronic activity, defining rights, enforcing penalties for misuse, and resolving conflicts in virtual environments.

What on earth is an AUP?

An Acceptable Use Policy (AUP) is a document defining the rules, restrictions, and expectations for using an organization's network, hardware, software, and internet services. It outlines what users are permitted and forbidden to do on the systems, protecting the organization from liability and security risks.

What is Plagiarism?

Plagiarism is the act of using someone else's words, ideas, data, or creative work and presenting them as your own without proper acknowledgment or citation.

It is considered an ethical violation in academic, professional, and creative fields, and in some contexts can lead to legal action for copyright infringement.

What is Academic Dishonesty?

Academic dishonesty (or academic misconduct) refers to any intentional or fraudulent action that undermines the integrity of the educational process, distorts the evaluation of academic performance, or provides an unfair advantage to a student.

What are the rules for AI generated content?

Schools and employers set their own Acceptable Use Policies (AUPs), ranging from full bans to permitted use as an ideation or proofreading aid with mandatory disclosure.

Passing off AI-generated writing, research, or code as your own original human work constitutes academic dishonesty or workplace fraud unless explicitly authorized.
